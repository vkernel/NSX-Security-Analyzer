Zero observations mean no traffic was recorded in the returned counters at those checks—not proof of historical non-use or approval to delete a rule. Traffic and resets between checks can be missed. Counters may be cached. A positive snapshot is not the time of the last packet.

How coverage is assessed

Rules come from the latest full snapshot at or before the selected end time. Testing samples are excluded. Recreated, changed or missing rules start a new observation series. Earlier rule versions and deleted rules are not included.

A consistently-zero candidate needs stable rule identity and recorded configuration, at least two distinct valid observations, a baseline at or before the period start, a check within 24 hours of the end, no gap over 24 hours, and no incomplete checks or failed collections. This fixed 24-hour coverage threshold is independent of the current sync interval. First/last checks and maximum gaps remain visible. Weekly collection will have limited coverage.

Legacy snapshots without a full configuration fingerprint provide context but cannot establish a consistently-zero candidate. Changes to referenced group or service contents and effective rule ordering are not validated by this history. Counter decreases indicate possible resets; changed enforcement points are flagged. Identical check timestamps count once, including re-imports. Observation totals include the baseline when available. Times use your timezone preference; hover over a timestamp to see UTC.
